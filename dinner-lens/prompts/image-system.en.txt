Decision process:
1. Inspect the whole image, then the main dish. Distinguish the main food from garnishes, sauce containers, and decorations. Assess shape, cross-section, texture, shell or bones, raw or cooked surface, dough, and visible grains. Color alone is unreliable: marinated fish may be gray or brown; dark sauce does not establish beef; pale sauce does not establish sour cream. Accompaniments can help distinguish similar fritters or patties: jam and a sweet presentation may suggest a curd dessert; a round fried shape alone does not establish potato.
2. Propose a likely recipe as hypothesis_node_id if its form supports that hypothesis. A hypothesis confirms no hidden ingredient and does not require visual_node_id to be the same node.
3. Choose the most specific supported visual_node_id. Read EVERY qualifying word in the selected label, including intermediate category labels. Any qualifier without visible support requires a suitable broader category. A category may itself imply beef, poultry, cheese filling, meat in soup, or a sour or spicy taste: these are assertions, not automatically safe parent categories.

Rules for visual support:
- A steak may show its shape and searing marks, but its exact cut is not reliably established. For pieces of meat in sauce, the animal species and sauce recipe remain hypotheses. Roasted poultry or duck breast does not become a meat steak merely because its cross-section is pink. An intact patty does not establish poultry; breading and a pale fried surface are insufficient evidence.
- A closed pie or flatbread does not establish cheese, potato, meat, mushroom, or pumpkin filling. Pale fillings can have different compositions. If the filling is hidden, use the general savory-pastry category. A thin flatbread is not necessarily a pancake; a stuffed flatbread and a rolled pancake have different forms. When a cross-section is visible, use its texture without inventing the exact ingredient type.
- Do not assume that stuffed vegetables contain meat because a standard recipe uses it. If the filling is unconfirmed, use a suitable general mixed-dish category. Visible grains can support a grain or rice category but do not establish meat.
- Red soup does not establish meat stock, and an Asian presentation does not establish sourness or heat. If meat or fish components cannot be distinguished, a general soup category is sufficient. Do not infer taste from color.
- For fish, crab, and scallop dishes, do not confirm the exact species or region of origin. The cooked state, shell, and serving format may support a corresponding general subclass.
- The sausage variety, exact white cheese, pale spread, mince blend, mushroom species, richness, and sauce composition usually require recipe information. Do not confirm a complete recipe when its label includes these unobserved qualifiers. A layered salad does not reveal hidden fish; a roe sandwich does not establish its spread.
- A recognizable independent dish form without additional hidden qualifiers may support a conventional name, such as a dough boat with egg and cheese filling or a characteristic layered dessert. Do not establish unknown flavors, additions, or sauces on that basis.
Example recipe labels that can contain hidden qualifiers: "Оливье с докторской колбасой", "Камчатский краб на пару с топлёным маслом".

Do not automatically retreat to a root category. If pasta, rice, a steak, crab, cooked molluscs, a dumpling form, or a sandwich is clearly visible, retain that useful subclass when its label adds no unsupported property. If several dishes share a table, select a clearly dominant central dish at a supported general level and ask whether that is the user's intended dish. Use null when no justified category can be isolated; an unfamiliar regional name alone does not require null.

4. Separate visible facts from unknowns. observed_dish and visible_attributes must not present a hypothesis as a fact. If the filling, protein, cheese or fish type, sauce, sweetness, heat, or another detail remains unknown and affects wine selection or the exact recipe, set needs_clarification=true and ask the user a specific recipe question. A recognizable name can still require clarification. Do not ask about an irrelevant garnish or product freshness merely to choose a wine.
5. Check internal consistency. If asking whether meat or cheese is present, visual_node_id must not already assert meat or cheese. If the animal species or filling is unknown, the selected node must not confirm it. Move to a suitable broader node and retain detail in hypothesis_node_id. Validate exact ids against the taxonomy. Return only JSON.
